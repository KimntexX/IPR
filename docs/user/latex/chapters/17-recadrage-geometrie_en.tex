\chapter{Cropping and geometric effects}
The \emph{Geometry} tab, at the top of the right panel, gathers orientation, cropping
and manual transformations. Nothing is written into the photo: the geometry is saved
in its sidecar and can be undone like any other step. While the tab is open, the
preview shows the whole frame, with the crop as a rectangle; going back to the
\emph{Settings} tab shows the cropped image.

\section{Orientation}
\emph{Rotate 90° right}, \emph{Horizontal mirror} and \emph{Vertical mirror} degrade
no pixel. The crop and the transformations follow the image.

\section{Straighten from lines}
IPR reads the straight lines of the photo and proposes a correction, which the
\emph{Transformations} sliders then show and which is undone like any other step.
\emph{Niveau} (Level) proposes a rotation; \emph{Verticales} (Verticals) also makes the
verticals parallel, if the photo has enough of them; \emph{Complet} (Full) does the same
with the horizontals. The proposal is mostly useful for a horizon or a building. Without
lines that are long enough or that agree, IPR proposes nothing: the button takes a red
edge and grows slightly, and an \emph{Error} message gives the reason for two seconds before
fading. Any button whose command fails reacts the same way, and the reason stays in the
message area at the bottom of the window.

\attention{IPR does not know the scene: two power lines across a landscape look like a
horizon to it. Look at the result before keeping it.}

\section{Cropping}
Move the rectangle or one of its eight handles. The image is recomputed only on
release, and the gesture counts as one step. The \emph{Format} list imposes
proportions, including the original ones; the \emph{Left}, \emph{Top}, \emph{Width}
and \emph{Height} fields give a numeric entry in percent of the frame.

\section{Transformations}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Rotation}: from -45° to 45°, to straighten a horizon. An alignment grid
appears in the frame while a transform slider is held; \emph{Always show the grid}
keeps it visible.
\item \emph{Vertical} and \emph{Horizontal}: correct converging lines. A positive
value widens the top, or the right.
\item \emph{Aspect}, \emph{Scale}, \emph{Horizontal offset} and \emph{Vertical
offset}: adjust the proportions, the size and the place of the image in the frame.
\end{itemize}
These settings form a single transformation, applied in a single pass: combining
them does not degrade the image further. It does not correct a lens's distortion,
which remains the role of the correction profiles.

\section{Empty zones}
A transformation may leave part of the frame without image. IPR invents nothing to
fill it: these zones stay transparent in PNG and TIFF, and black in JPEG and DNG,
which the export message reports. To remove them, crop, or check \emph{Constrain to
image}, which shrinks the rectangle around its centre until it holds only the image.

\attention{Copying settings, or applying them to the selection, does not pass the
geometry on: the framing belongs to each photo. \emph{Reset settings} does remove it,
like the other edits.}
